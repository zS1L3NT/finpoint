Why Finpoint?

So you’ve decided to take charge of your spending, maybe after noticing your bank balance running dry faster than you expected. Welcome to Finpoint. This guide walks you through everything Finpoint can do, so you can use all of it, or just the parts you need. Track several accounts, or just a few daily payments from one. Finpoint works either way. But your banking app already lists every payment, so what’s missing?

Your bank can tell you where every dollar went. What it can’t tell you is what each payment was really for.

Let’s say you spent $80 at the supermarket: $50 on groceries, and $30 on a birthday gift for a friend. Your banking app shows one $80 payment, and nothing more. But to you, those are two very different things, and you’d like to label them that way.

Or let’s say you paid $90 for dinner with a friend, and they sent their $60 share back a week later. Your banking app shows two payments, a week apart, so you’d have to scroll past everything in between to get the full picture. But to you, it’s really one $30 dinner, and you’d like to label it that way.

Finpoint was built to close that gap. It keeps your bank activity exactly as your bank reports it, and lets you label it in your own words: groceries, $50. A gift, $30. Your share of dinner, $30.

That’s the whole idea of Finpoint: adding labels to your bank activity, one or many, simple or detailed. Once your spending is labelled, it finally makes sense.

So how do you use Finpoint? It comes down to just two things: bring in your bank activity, and label it. Everything else falls into place from there.

To do that, you’ll need three ideas. Statements: your bank activity, exactly as your bank reports it. Records: your labels for that activity. And Allocations: the links between the two.

————————

Statements, Records and Allocations

A Statement is one line of bank activity, exactly as your bank reports it, like that $80 supermarket payment. Finpoint never lets you edit the Statements your bank gives you, so you can trust them to match your bank. Well, almost always. We’ll get to that.

A Record is your label for what happened, like the groceries for $50, and the gift for $30.

An Allocation links a Statement to a Record, and says how much of the Statement belongs to it. Here, $50 of the supermarket payment is allocated to Groceries, and the other $30 to the gift.

So one Statement can be split across several Records. That’s how you untangle a mixed shopping trip, or a bill that covers two different things.

It works the other way too: several Statements can be combined into one Record. Your $90 dinner payment and your friend’s $60 transfer both belong to one Dinner Record. It shows what you really spent: $30.

Most of the time, though, it’s simpler: one Statement, labelled by one Record. A $12 lunch you had on your own is one Statement, and one Lunch Record. No need to overcomplicate things.

And when things get messier, Finpoint keeps up. Say you bought $80 of groceries and a $40 gift in one checkout, but paid $60 on each of two cards. That’s two Statements and two Records, linked by three Allocations.

We won’t need anything that tangled in this guide. It’s just to show that Finpoint is as flexible as you need it to be. Enough examples. Let’s get you familiar with Finpoint itself.

————————

Trying Finpoint first

On your first visit, you’ll be greeted by an empty Dashboard. Since you don’t have any data yet, choose Try demo data. It loads three bank accounts and five months of Records, so you can see what a lived-in Finpoint looks like.

When you’re done exploring, clear the demo from the Data page, with Clear all data. Then you’re ready to import your own bank activity.

————————

Importing your bank activity

Many banks let you download your activity as a file, called an export, from their website or app. Finpoint doesn’t connect to your bank directly, so this file is how your bank activity gets in.

Import the file exactly as your bank gave it to you. Finpoint reads each bank’s export as it is, so an edited file might not import correctly.

For now, Finpoint reads exports from DBS, OCBC, UOB and Revolut, as a CSV file or an Excel spreadsheet, depending on the bank.

You don’t need to sort your files by account. Each export says which bank account it came from, so Finpoint files its Statements under a matching Account for you. Import exports from two DBS accounts, and you’ll get two Accounts in Finpoint, each with its own Statements.

Revolut is the exception: its exports don’t say which account they came from. For those, Finpoint will ask you which Account the file belongs to, or to create a new one, with an ID and a name.

1. Let’s import your first file. Download a CSV or Excel export from your bank, once for each account you want to track.

2. Then open the Importer from the sidebar, choose your bank, and add your files. You can add several at once, except for Revolut, which takes one file at a time.

3. Choose Import, and Finpoint will tell you how many new Statements came in.

4. And as we mentioned, a Revolut export needs one more choice before you import it: pick its Account, or create a new one with an ID and a name.

5. Your new Statements now wait in Workflows for you to label, so that’s where we’re heading next.

————————

Workflows

Workflows is your to-do list, and it’s where you’ll spend most of your time in Finpoint. Its first tab, Allocate to Records, lists every Statement that still needs a label, newest first.

Once a Statement is fully labelled, it leaves this list. So an empty list means you’re all caught up, at least until your next import.

The other two tabs help you tidy up your imported Statements. We’ll cover them later.

To look things up, use the Records and Statements pages. They list every Record and every Statement you have, with filters to help you find anything. Workflows is different: it only shows the Statements that still need you.

————————

One to one: your first Record

1. Let’s label that $12 solo lunch from earlier. In Workflows, click the lunch Statement to select it. A bar will appear at the top, with what you can do next.

2. Choose Create Record. We’ll cover the other options later. The form opens with the Statement’s $12 already allocated, and the amount and date filled in for you.

3. Give the Record a title, like Lunch, and pick a Category, like Dining Out. A Category says what the money was for, and it will matter on your Dashboard later.

Under Analytics, you’ll also see Treatment and Monthly Budget. Leave them as they are for now: your Category fills them in. They’re more advanced, so we’ll cover them later.

4. Check that everything looks right, then choose Create Record at the bottom to save. The lunch Statement is now fully allocated, so it leaves the list.

————————

One to many: splitting a payment

1. Now let’s split the $80 supermarket trip. Select it in Workflows, and choose Create Record.

2. Call it Groceries, and set both the Record’s amount and its Allocation to −$50. The Record is what you spent, and the Allocation is how much of this $80 payment it uses. The other $30 stays unlabelled, for the next Record.

3. Choose Create Record. Because only part of it is labelled, the supermarket Statement stays in the list, showing the $30 left.

4. Select it again, and create a second Record, Birthday gift. Finpoint fills in the $30 that’s left for you.

5. Choose Create Record, and the supermarket Statement leaves the list.

It’s now fully labelled by the two Records we just created: $50 of groceries, and a $30 gift.

————————

Many to one: combining payments

1. Now let’s combine the two payments from that shared dinner. Select both Statements: the $90 you paid, and the $60 your friend Sam sent back. The bar adds them up: $90 out and $60 in, so $30 out.

2. Choose Create Record, and call it Dinner. Both Statements are already allocated, and the Record comes to $30: what dinner really cost you.

Choose Create Record, and both Statements are labelled by a single Record. Your spending now shows the $30 you really spent, not $90.

————————

Where your numbers come from

We’ve spent some time creating Records. So how does that help you understand your money? With a few Records saved, your Dashboard starts to fill in, because everything on it is calculated from Records, never directly from Statements.

That’s why importing alone doesn’t change your Dashboard. A Statement only counts once a Record labels it.

And because it uses your labels, the Dashboard shows what you really spent: $30 on dinner, not $90, and groceries apart from the gift.

There’s a lot more on the Dashboard. Scroll through and explore it at your own pace. We’ll come back to reading it in more depth later.

————————

Pending Records

Your Dashboard is only as up to date as your labels. But sometimes you know about spending before your bank shows all of it, and you’d rather label it now than try to remember it later.

Say you paid $200 for two concert tickets, and your friend Jo will pay you back $100 next week. You can already create the Record for your $100 share, from the $200 payment.

The amounts don’t match yet: $100 against $200. Finpoint warns you, and saves the Record as Pending, which means it isn’t settled yet. Your $100 share already counts.

Pending Records are marked in your Records list, so nothing gets forgotten.

When Jo’s $100 arrives, select it in Workflows and choose Allocate to record. Finpoint will suggest the Pending Record it completes.

The Record now combines both payments. Save it, and because the amounts add up, it’s no longer Pending.

So a Record is Pending for one of two reasons. Its amounts don’t add up yet, like the concert. Or one of its Statements is still Pending at the bank. Either way, Pending means the Record isn’t fully confirmed yet.

————————

Pending Statements

Remember how a Statement matches your bank, almost always? This is the exception. A Pending Statement is bank activity that isn’t confirmed yet. Some imports, like Revolut’s, bring card payments in as Pending. And if your bank takes days to show a payment, you can add one yourself, and edit it until the real one arrives, so your month stays complete.

1. On the Statements page, choose Create Pending Statement. Pick the account, then enter the amount and a short description.

2. It appears in Workflows, marked Pending. Label it like any other Statement: here, a burger with Mia, under Dining Out. Save it, and the Record counts straight away, but stays Pending while its Statement does.

3. When the confirmed Statement arrives in a later import, open Replace Pending in Workflows. Pick the Pending Statement, and Finpoint will suggest the imported Statements it could become.

4. Review the swap, and choose Replace pending Statement. The Allocations move to the confirmed Statement, the Pending one disappears, and nothing is counted twice.

————————

Fixing duplicate Statements

Imports can go wrong in one more way. Sometimes a bank changes a payment’s description between exports. Import both files, and the same payment arrives twice, so you might label it twice.

Finpoint spots Statements with the same account, day and amount, and marks them as possible duplicates. Open Fix Duplicates in Workflows to review them.

1. If they really are two payments, choose Not duplicates. Otherwise, choose the Statement to keep, then Review deletion.

2. Finpoint shows you what will happen before anything is deleted. Allocations follow the Statement you keep, so your Records stay labelled.

————————

Finding and fixing Records

Sooner or later, you’ll want to look something up, like what that dinner with Sam really cost. Open Records, then search by title, or filter by date, Category or amount.

1. Open a Record to see its Statements, then choose Edit Record to change any field, or the Allocations behind it.

The Statements page works the same way for your bank activity, and each Account has a page of its own.

And if you always look at the same period, set default filters in Settings, so Records and Allocate to Records open on that range.

————————

Categories

We’ve been picking Categories all along. They’re the most important choice you make on a Record: they say what it was for, and your Dashboard breaks your spending down by them.

Lunch and dinner with friends are Dining Out. The supermarket run is Groceries. The birthday gift goes under Gifts & Donations. Finpoint starts you with a sensible set, and you can rename them, add your own, or nest one under another, like Coffee under Dining Out.

Each Category also carries two defaults: a Treatment, and a Monthly Budget. That’s why you could leave those fields alone earlier: your Category fills them in. We’ll look at both, right after one more time-saver.

————————

Statement rules

Some payments come back every month, like your phone bill, and labelling them again and again gets old. A Statement rule recognises them, and fills in the Record for you when you create it. It never creates Records on its own.

1. Open Rules, and choose New rule. Give it a name, and the text to look for in the description. Finpoint shows you the Statements it matches.

2. Then choose what it fills in: a title, a Category, even People or a Location. Autofill puts the value straight into the field. Suggest offers it as a one-click suggestion instead.

Next time you create a Record from a matching Statement, the autofilled fields are ready, and the suggestions are one click away. You can still change any of them.

————————

Treatments

Now, those two defaults. The first is Treatment. It decides how the Dashboard counts a Record: as income, as spending, as saving, or not at all.

Your salary is Income. Lunch and groceries are Spending. A refund is Spending too, because it lowers what you spent. Putting $500 into an investment account is Saving / investment. And moving money between two accounts you track is a Transfer, which isn’t counted at all.

On the Dashboard, Income and Spending make up your totals and charts. Saving and investment are kept out of them, in their own section further down, so saving never looks like spending. And Transfers don’t appear at all.

Your Category sets the treatment for you. Change it on a single Record only when that Record is an exception.

————————

Monthly Budgets

The second default is a Monthly Budget. It answers a different question: am I on track? A Monthly Budget is a group of spending you plan for. Finpoint starts you with four: Daily for everyday spending, Recurring for bills like rent and your phone, Irregular for one-off costs, and Travel. Each gets its own amount every month.

Daily is the one to watch. It holds everyday spending, like food and transport, and the Dashboard forecasts where it’s heading by the end of the month.

1. To give Daily an amount, choose Edit beside it on the Dashboard, and set a Monthly amount, say $500.

2. The Amount rule decides which months that amount covers. Override one month changes this month only. Override default applies from this month onward.

The Dashboard then shows how the month is going against the plan. A Monthly Budget doesn’t move or set aside any money. It’s a comparison, nothing more.

————————

Reading the Overview

With a plan in place, the Overview, the Dashboard’s first tab, becomes your monthly check-in. Its top answers the big questions: what came in, what you spent, and your surplus, which is the difference. Each number is compared with the last two months, at the same point in the month.

Choose a Monthly Budget at the top, and the spending charts narrow to it. Cards whose names start with Total ignore that choice, and always show everything.

The Spending forecast chart shows this month so far, where it’s heading, and the months before, so you can see early if you’re running ahead.

Further down, Categories this month and their trend show what changed, and Biggest changes calls out the largest moves.

The calendar colours each day by how much you spent. Pick a day to see its Records.

————————

Monthly Records

When a number on the Overview surprises you, Monthly Records shows what’s behind it. It sits next to the Overview: every Record in the month, grouped by day, with each day’s total.

Click any part of a chart on the Overview, and Monthly Records opens on just those Records.

And at the bottom of the Overview, Review this month links to anything that needs you: Pending Records, Pending Statements, and spending that isn’t in any Monthly Budget, so no plan is tracking it.

————————

Budgets for trips and events

Everything so far works month by month. A Budget, with no Monthly in front, is different: it has its own start and end, like a week in Bali, or a wedding. You see the whole trip in one place, even when it spans two months.

1. Create one with a name, an amount and its dates.

2. Automatic attach adds every Record dated inside the period, including income and transfers. To choose the Records yourself, turn it off, and attach them from the Budget’s page.

A Budget is its own small dashboard for that period: what you’ve spent, what’s left, and your pace. Its Records still count on your monthly Dashboard as usual.

————————

Backups and Google Drive

You’ve put real work into labelling your money, so it’s worth knowing where it lives. Finpoint runs in your browser, and keeps your data on this device. There’s no Finpoint database, and your finances never pass through our servers, so we never see them.

That means keeping a copy is up to you, and there are two ways. The first is a backup file. Download it from the Data page whenever you like, and restore it later, on this device or a new one. Restoring replaces this browser’s data with the backup.

The second is Google Drive. Connect it once, and Finpoint keeps a copy in a hidden folder in your own Drive, automatically, so your devices stay in step.

If the same data changed on two devices, Finpoint asks which copy to keep, and keeps that whole copy, never a mix of both, so your Records always add up.

Either way, your data only goes to places you control.

————————

Your routine

Here’s a routine that works. Import your latest bank exports. Fix any duplicates, and label what’s new in Workflows. Check anything Pending. Glance at your Dashboard. And keep a backup.

With Google Drive connected, that happens automatically. If not, download a backup file every so often.

And whenever you’re unsure, choose Help at the top of any page. It lists the guides for that page, step by step.
